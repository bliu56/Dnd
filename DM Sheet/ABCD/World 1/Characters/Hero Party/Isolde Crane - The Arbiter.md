### Background

- Born into a minor noble house with deep ties to the Church going back four generations
- Every Crane woman has served the Goddess in some capacity — priests, oracles, seers
- Was placed in the Church as a child and has spent her entire life being told she is the Goddess's most gifted vessel in a generation
- Believed it immediately and has never stopped
- Joined the Hero party because she decided the Goddess directed her there and that the Hero's mission is the most worthy cause currently alive
- Everyone else's cause is lesser by comparison

### Personality

- Serene on the surface, ruthless underneath
- Speaks softly, moves gracefully, passes judgment constantly and without hesitation
- Decides who is worth healing based entirely on whether she personally deems them worthy in the Goddess's eyes
- Has let people die before — considers it the Goddess's will and sleeps fine afterward
- Gets along with Kael because he is noble and the Hero — both marks of divine favor in her eyes
- Tolerates Seraphine because results matter even when the person producing them is godless
- Barely acknowledges Gareth as a spiritual presence worth engaging with

### Fighting Style

Never in danger, always watching. She positions herself at the edge of every fight with perfect calm and heals exactly who she decides deserves it exactly when she decides they deserve it. She has never been in a hurry about it. If someone goes down before she gets to them that is information about their worthiness, not a failure on her part.

### Opinion of the Players

- Will assess them spiritually and morally within the first meeting
- If they fall short of her standard she will heal them in combat because they are useful, not because they matter
- If they meet her standard she becomes marginally warmer — which still isn't warm

### Opinion of the Demon King

- Considers [[Tristan]] an affront to the Goddess personally
- The public's sympathy toward him is proof of moral corruption on a civilizational scale
- Has no interest in understanding him — understanding implies he is worth understanding

### Bonus Actions

- **Sacred Blessing:** At the start of each of Isolde's turns she generates one stack of Sacred Favor, max 6. As a bonus action she can distribute any number of her current stacks among a number of allies she can see equal to her Wisdom modifier (currently 4). Each stack distributed to an ally gives them the following benefits until the start of Isolde's next turn:
  - +1 to attack rolls and saving throws per stack
  - +1d4 healing received per stack whenever they regain HP from any source
  - Stacks distributed to allies are consumed at the start of Isolde's next turn. Undistributed stacks carry over. At max stacks (6) Isolde must distribute at least one stack on her next turn or the excess burns away.
- **Healing Word (4/Day):** Isolde calls out to one worthy ally within 60 ft, restoring 1d6+4 HP.
  - *"The Goddess sees you."*

### Reactions

- **Protective Grace:** When a worthy ally within 30 ft. is hit by an attack, Isolde imposes disadvantage on the attack roll as a reaction.

### Story Notes (DM Only)

- Withheld Mercy is the most dangerous thing about her — not her damage, not her healing, but the fact that she will let a player die if she has deemed them unworthy and feel completely justified
- The moment a player realizes she made that choice will be significant — reward however the table reacts
- She has never had her judgment questioned by someone she couldn't dismiss — that changes the moment the players push back seriously
- Her assessment of the Demon King as an affront to the Goddess will not survive contact with the reality of what he has actually done for common people — whether she ever reckons with that is up to the table

### Story Notes (DM Only) — Optional Progression

Isolde warms to those who share her faith. Not friends — fellow believers. Entirely optional.

| Stage | Trigger | Response |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Noticed** | A player prays, invokes the Goddess, or shows religious devotion in her presence | A long, silent look. Then a nod. "...You pray. Most people don't." |
| **2 — Tested** | She challenges their faith — puts them in a situation where devotion costs something | "The Goddess tests those she loves. Prove you love her more than your fear." |
| **3 — Proven** | The player chooses faith over pragmatism — spares when killing was easier, endures without complaint, gives up something valuable because it was right | "You passed. I was watching. The Goddess was watching too." |
| **4 — Accepted** | She considers them a fellow servant of the Light | "Rise. You are not below me. You stand beside me. The Light recognizes the Light." |

**The understanding:** She doesn't want friends. She wants fellow believers. The moment a player treats faith as real — not a tool, not an excuse — she stops being their judge and starts being their ally.
