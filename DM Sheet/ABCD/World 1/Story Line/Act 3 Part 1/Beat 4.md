## Option A — The Hero Party (West)

Key points:

- Kael, Gareth, Seraphine, Isolde — alive, intact, moving with total certainty toward the Demon King directly
- Their plan is exactly what it sounds like: find the Demon King, kill him, war over. No nuance, no strategy, no contingency
- They are not looking for input. They are not looking for allies. They are looking for the Demon King and they have decided they have everything they need
- The players arrive with information that complicates that plan — the Ashen Vale, what Corvin died to pass on, what the window closing actually means
- Getting the Hero Party to listen is the challenge. Kael does not take information seriously from people he has decided are beneath him. Seraphine will only engage if the argument is airtight. Gareth doesn't think strategy is his problem. Isolde will assess whether the players are worth listening to before she hears a word they say.
- The players have to convince them — not just that the information is real, but that it matters more than the plan they already have
- Kael is the decision maker. If Kael doesn't move, nobody moves. But Kael responds to two things: authority and being proven wrong in a way he cannot dismiss. Players have to find their angle.
- Seraphine is the most likely to actually engage with the information on its merits — if the argument is logical and the evidence holds she will listen even if she doesn't like the source. She may be the key to moving Kael.
- If players succeed: the Hero Party redirects toward the Ashen Vale, grudgingly, with an attitude that makes cooperation uncomfortable but functional
- If players fail: the Hero Party continues west. Whatever happens to them is on the timeline now and the players know it

## Option B — [[John (Act 3)|John]] and [[Luna (Act 3)|Luna]] (East)

Key points:

- John and Luna were at the Church when it fell. They got out. They have been moving since with whatever survivors they could pull with them.
- They are not broken by it. They are the reason the people behind them aren't broken by it. That has a cost that neither of them is currently acknowledging.
- When they see the players the held-together thing loosens slightly — not dramatically, not a collapse, just a visible exhale from two people who have been carrying something heavy and have suddenly seen a familiar face

---

**The Reunion**

Luna's relief comes out first and fastest — warm, immediate, slightly overwhelming, the specific emotion of someone who has been worrying about people for two years and has just found out the worrying was unnecessary. She does not try to hide it.

John says something short. It means more than it sounds like. He does not elaborate.

Give this room before anything else. They were at the Church. They got out. The players just walked through what they left behind. Nobody needs to say any of that out loud for it to be present in the room.

---

**What They Went Through**

They don't give a full account unless asked. What they will say if pushed: the attack came fast and it knew exactly where to hit. John got Luna out. Luna got John out. Some of the survivors behind them are here because one or both of them went back in when they shouldn't have. Neither of them will say that part — someone else in the group will, quietly, if players talk to the survivors long enough.

---

**Their Plan**

Since getting out they have been doing the only thing that made sense — moving through the regions the war has touched, pulling out survivors, recruiting anyone willing to help. Not an army. Just people. It has been slow and dangerous and they have kept doing it anyway. Their goal has always been the same underneath all of it: get enough together to matter.

---

**The Information**

When the players share what Corvin died to pass on — the Ashen Vale, the window, what the Demon King has been moving — John and Luna take it seriously immediately. They listen, they ask the right questions, and then they go quiet for a moment while they think.

The debate that follows is genuine and unresolved:

_Go to the Hero Party and request their help toward the Ashen Vale_ — more firepower, better odds, but the Hero Party is difficult and the clock may not allow for the detour

_Go themselves_ — faster, cleaner, but their group is small and whatever is at the Ashen Vale warranted a month of demon patrol rerouting

Neither option is obviously right. John leans toward moving themselves — he does not trust the Hero Party's judgment and does not want to lose time. Luna sees the numbers problem and does not like it. They will genuinely listen when players weigh in.

---

**The Artifact Rumor**

Somewhere in the debate Luna mentions it — almost as an aside. A rumor, old and unverified, of an ancient Hero artifact. Something from a previous Hero's time, lost or hidden or forgotten. The location is not far — a slight detour, maybe half a day off the direct path to the Ashen Vale.

The catch: only a Hero can use it. Luna does not say this looking at John. She doesn't need to. The implication sits in the room without anyone naming it.

John's response is immediate and flat — he is not the Hero, the artifact is not his concern, they should focus on what they know rather than chasing rumors. He means this. He also does not quite look at the mark on his hand when he says it.

Whether anyone pushes back on that is up to the players.

---

**The Decisions on the Table**

Three things to weigh by the end of the conversation:

- **The Ashen Vale** — the window is closing, the information is real, something is there that matters
- **The Hero Party** — west, reachable, a resource that comes with significant complications
- **The Artifact** — a half day detour, unverified, only useful to someone nobody in the room has officially acknowledged yet

None of these resolve cleanly into each other. That is the point.

---

**Point Moments (DM Only)**

- Luna's face when she sees the players — the two years of worry releasing all at once
- John's short thing that means more than it sounds like in that first moment
- A survivor quietly telling players what John and Luna actually did getting people out — reward whoever talks to the survivors long enough to hear it
- The debate between John and Luna feeling like a real disagreement between two people who respect each other and still see it differently
- A player pushing back on John about the artifact and watching him not have a good answer
- A player asking Luna directly what she thinks John is — reward whoever asks and whatever Luna does with it
- The moment the group has to commit to a direction with the Ashen Vale window pressing against everything else
