**Old Mill Road**

_Type: Travel / Atmosphere_

The faster path. The road is old and well-worn, cut through terrain that used to see regular traffic before the war emptied it. It moves quickly — the ground is flat, the route is direct, and a group moving with purpose can make good time. The problem is that the same qualities that make it fast make it exposed. There is no cover. Anyone on this road can be seen from a distance and there is nowhere to go if something finds them.

The players will feel the exposure without anything necessarily happening immediately. The road is quiet in the specific way of somewhere that used to be busy. Abandoned carts, a waypost with the Church seal defaced, a farmhouse with the door open and nobody inside. The war came through here and kept moving and left the emptiness behind.

One encounter on the road — a demon patrol moving in the opposite direction. Not hunting the players specifically, just moving. The exposed terrain means the players see them coming and have time to decide: hide in what little cover exists, let the patrol pass and hope they aren't noticed, or engage. There is not much to hide behind. The decision has a cost either way.

_Point Moments:_

- A player notices the defaced waypost and says something
- A player makes the call on the patrol and owns the consequences
- Someone clocks how exposed they are and says it out loud

**The Waystone**

_Type: Infiltration / Combat / Choice_

The Waystone is a fortified relay point the demons captured early in the campaign. Physically it is an old stone structure — a watchtower base with a signaling platform at the top, built generations ago to pass messages across the region. The demons recognized its value immediately. From the platform they can send and receive signals across a significant stretch of territory. Every demon force operating in the region checks in through the Waystone. It is the nervous system of their local operation.

The players need to get past it or through it. The road runs directly past the structure and the demons have clear sightlines in both directions.

**What's there:**

- 12 demon soldiers holding the position
- A demon signal operator on the platform — not a fighter, but the most important person in the building
- A signaling apparatus on the platform that the players may or may not care about

**The Options:**

_Go around_ — possible but costs time. The terrain off the road is rough and slow. The players chose this path to save time. Going around eats most of that advantage. No combat, but the window to the Ashen Vale narrows.

_Sneak past_ — the sightlines are good but not perfect. A DC 15 group Stealth check. On a success the players slip past during a shift change or a moment of inattention. On a failure they are spotted and the alarm goes up — not just soldiers, but a signal sent from the platform before the players can stop it. Something will be waiting for them further down the road.

_Fight through_ — viable but loud. 12 soldiers plus the operator. If the operator gets to the platform before the players stop them, a signal goes out. The players have to prioritize the platform.

_Destroy the apparatus_ — if players fight through or find another way in, they can destroy the signaling equipment. This is not required but has consequences — demon coordination in the region degrades, which matters in ways the players may not see immediately but the DM should track.

_Make a deal_ — the demons here are soldiers, not generals. They can be negotiated with if players have something worth offering. This is a long shot but not impossible depending on the table.

**The Signal Problem:** If a signal goes out — whether from a failed stealth check, a fight that takes too long, or a player decision — something is waiting for the players at the next location. Not immediately, but it is coming. The DM should track this and deploy it when it fits.
