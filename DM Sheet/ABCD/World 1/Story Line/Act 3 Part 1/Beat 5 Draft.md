_Type: Travel / Combat / Pressure_

---

**The Feel**

Three days north, past the twin peaks. The road does not get easier. Demon patrol activity increases the further north they go — not because the demons are hunting the players specifically, not yet, but because this is Demon King territory now and the patrols are constant and overlapping. There is no clean stretch of road. There is no safe camp. The pressure is ambient and unrelenting and it does not stop when the players stop.

Every decision about where to rest, when to move, how to handle what's in the way is made against the background of a window that is closing on an unknown timeline.

---

**Part 1 — The First Day: Occupied Roads**

The roads this far north are demon controlled. Not barricaded, not fortified — just used. Demon patrols move through regularly, and the players are moving against that grain.

_Encounter 1 — The Patrol_ A standard demon patrol, eight to ten soldiers, moving in the opposite direction. Not hunting the players — just moving. The players have to decide: avoid, ambush, or find a way through. Avoiding is possible but costs time and terrain. Ambushing is viable but noise is a problem — more patrols are within earshot. Finding a way through requires either deception or something creative.

This sets the tone for the whole journey — every encounter has multiple solutions and none of them are free.

_Encounter 2 — The Terrain_ The road passes through a narrow gorge. Inside it the players find evidence of a recent battle — Church soldiers, all dead, killed within the last day or two. The gorge is also the only reasonable path forward. It is also where something has been denning — not a demon, just a large predatory creature that has been feeding on the bodies and is not interested in sharing. Players have to move through without triggering it or deal with it directly.

_Rest — No Safe Camp_ When the players stop to rest they will find no location that feels secure. Patrol sounds in the distance, movement in the treeline, the specific quality of terrain that belongs to someone else. A full rest is possible but costs time and nerve. A short rest is safer but leaves everyone depleted going into the next day.

---

**Part 2 — The Second Day: The Mistweaver's Territory**

The terrain changes. The trees grow denser, older, darker. The players are moving into territory that belongs to the Mistweaver now — one of [[Tristan]]'s two new Demon Generals, the corrupted creature from the [[dnd/DM Sheet/ABCD/World 1/Locations/Evergreen Forest/Story Notes (DM Only)|Evergreen Forest]], larger and more deliberate than it was before.

It does not attack immediately. It announces itself gradually — spores in the air, visibility dropping in patches, the disorientation arriving before the creature does. Players who fought or fled it before will recognize what is happening. Players who didn't will figure it out the hard way.

_Encounter 3 — The Mistweaver's Territory_ The Mistweaver is not trying to kill the players. It is herding them. It wants them disoriented, separated, moving in the direction it chooses rather than the direction they intend. The actual combat encounter only triggers if players push into its core territory or if they have no other option.

Running is valid. Finding the edge of its territory and moving around it costs time but avoids the fight entirely. Fighting it directly is brutal and loud and will draw demon patrols to the noise.

If the artifact detour is happening, the Mistweaver's territory sits uncomfortably close to the detour path — players may have to pass through it twice.

_Encounter 4 — The Demon Hunting Party_ Someone has noticed the players by now. A demon hunting party — smaller than a full patrol, faster, specifically tasked — has been dispatched to intercept them. This is not a random encounter. These demons know approximately where the players are and are moving to cut them off.

The hunting party is CR 4-5 individually, six total, with a demon commander at CR 7. They fight smart — they try to separate, to flank, to use the terrain. They are not interested in prisoners.

---

**Part 3 — The Third Day: The Twin Peaks**

The twin peaks are visible from a distance — two identical rock formations rising above the treeline, the landmark Corvin described. The Ashen Vale is on the other side. The players are close.

The third day is the hardest.

_Encounter 5 — The Liger's Warning_ The Liger appears. Not to fight — to be seen. It emerges from the treeline ahead of the players, looks at them for a long moment, and then is gone back into the trees. It does not attack. It does not need to. Its presence means Tristan knows exactly where they are and has decided to let them know he knows.

If [[John (Act 3)|John]] is with the party his jaw tightens. He does not say anything.

_Encounter 6 — The Pass_ The path between the twin peaks is the only way through to the Ashen Vale. It is also narrow, exposed, and currently occupied by a demon garrison — not a patrol, a stationary force. Thirty soldiers, a fortified position, two entrances both watched.

This is the setpiece of the journey. Players have to get through. How is entirely up to them — assault, stealth, deception, finding a third path through the peaks that isn't the road. If John and [[Luna (Act 3)|Luna]] are present they have opinions. If the Hero Party is present Kael will immediately advocate for a frontal assault and will be wrong about how easy it will be.

Getting through the pass means the Ashen Vale is ahead. Whatever is there is waiting.

---

**Artifact Detour — If Players Choose It**

A half day off the main path, through the edge of the Mistweaver's territory. The location is an old ruin — pre-war, pre-current conflict, something from a previous Hero's time that has been sitting undisturbed long enough that the forest has started to reclaim it.

The artifact is inside. Getting to it requires navigating the ruin — not a dungeon, just an old structure with old problems. Something still active inside, not hostile but not welcoming either, that responds to the presence of a Hero Candidate differently than it responds to everyone else.

John will not go in first. He will go in last if he goes in at all. Whatever happens inside when he gets close to the artifact is between him, the mark on his hand, and whoever is watching.

---

**The Pressure Throughout**

The window is always present. Every detour, every fight that takes longer than expected, every rest that gets cut short — all of it feeds the same quiet question underneath everything else. Players will not know if they made it in time until they arrive. That does not go away.
