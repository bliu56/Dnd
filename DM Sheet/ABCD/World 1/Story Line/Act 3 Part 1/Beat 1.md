_Type: Hook / Combat / Introduction_

---
**Setting the Scene**

The players hear it before they see it. Steel on steel, then less steel, then mostly screaming. When they arrive the picture is already decided. A Church patrol being taken apart by a demon squad. Not a fight. A conclusion. The demons are efficient and unhurried about it.

Let players try to help if they want. It changes nothing — the soldiers are already gone by the time any intervention matters. The demons are CR 2-3, dangerous in numbers, not interested in the players unless directly engaged. Once the patrol is finished they disengage. They were not sent here for the players.

---

**The Soldier**

His name is Corvin. Mid-thirties, career Church soldier, nothing remarkable about him except that he is still moving when everyone else isn't. Crossbow bolt in his side that he has stopped thinking about. He sees the players and comes toward them with the specific purposefulness of someone who has one thing left to do.

He doesn't ask for help. He asks if they're Church affiliated — or close enough. Then he talks fast.

_"There's a place called the Ashen Vale. Three days north, past the twin peaks. The Demon King has been moving something there — not soldiers, not supplies. Something else. I don't know what. But every demon patrol in the last month has been routing through it or away from it. Whatever is happening there — it won't stay open long. I don't know how long. I just know it won't."_

He makes them repeat it back. Ashen Vale. Three days north. Past the twin peaks. Won't stay open long.

Then the demons find him. It is quick. He doesn't run.

**The Liger Arrives**

Before the demons finish Corvin off, something else arrives. The demons that were still present go still — not attacking, not moving. Waiting. The Liger emerges from the treeline or the smoke or the dark, whatever fits the scene. It is not what it was in the [[dnd/DM Sheet/ABCD/World 1/Locations/Evergreen Forest/Story Notes (DM Only)|Evergreen Forest]]. Two years under [[Tristan (Act 1)]]'s influence have done visible things to it. It is larger, denser, the corruption running through it in a way that looks deliberate rather than incidental. It was sent here specifically. For Corvin.

Corvin knows what this means. He looks at it and then he looks at the players.

_"Go."_

Not a request. He has already turned back toward the Liger by the time he says it. He is not under any illusion about what happens next. He just needs them moving before it does.

If players hesitate he says it again, quieter this time.

_"You have the information. That's what matters. Go."_

The Liger does not immediately pursue the players — Corvin is its target and Corvin is making himself one. Whatever Corvin does to delay it buys the players exactly enough time to be out of reach. They will hear what happens behind them. They do not need to see it.

---

**Running the Death**

Don't hide that he's dying — let players feel it coming. The bolt, the pallor, the way he keeps one hand pressed to his side. If players try to stabilize him he waves them off — there isn't time and he knows it. He doesn't know how long the window is. He says so if asked. He just knows it closes.

The point is not the death. The point is that he chose to spend his last minutes making sure the information didn't die with him.

---

**What Players Leave With**

- The name: Ashen Vale
- The direction: three days north, past the twin peaks
- The pressure: a window that is closing on an unknown timeline
- The unspoken question: what is the Demon King moving there that isn't soldiers or supplies

---

**The Tension Going Forward**

Players have no way of knowing how much time they have. Every stop on the road to the Church is a choice they are making without knowing its cost. The crater city, the thriving village, the side stop — all still there, all still worth engaging with. But now each one sits underneath a question nobody can answer: is this worth it. They will not know until they arrive at the Ashen Vale whether they made it in time. That ambiguity is the pressure. It never goes away.

---

**Point Moments (DM Only)**

- A player tries to save Corvin and realizes too late it was never possible
- A player asks his name before he dies
- A player asks how long the window is and watches Corvin not know the answer
- A player immediately starts asking what the Demon King would be moving there — reward the instinct
- A player looks back at the dead patrol after Corvin dies and says nothing
