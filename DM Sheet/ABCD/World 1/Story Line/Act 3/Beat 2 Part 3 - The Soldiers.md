_Type: Combat / Moral Choice_

---
**Setting the Scene**

The players find the camp off the road — a rough thing, no real structure, the kind of camp made by people who stopped caring about doing it properly. A dozen Church soldiers in various states of disrepair. Armor that hasn't been maintained, weapons that have. A commanding officer named **Ser Aldous Crane** — fifties, career military, the kind of man who has followed orders his entire life and is now giving them with the same mechanical certainty even though the institution behind them has largely ceased to exist.

He is not cruel. He is not enjoying this. He has simply decided that the war requires what it requires and Millhaven has what his men need to keep moving.

---

**The Encounter**

The soldiers clock the players before the players clock the camp. By the time the party arrives Crane is already standing at the edge of it waiting for them — not aggressive, just present. He looks at their supplies with the specific assessment of someone who has already made a decision and is now calculating the details.

He is polite about it initially. Church authority, war effort, requisition of supplies for active military personnel. He has the language for it. He uses it cleanly and without apology.

If players comply he takes what he needs and tells them Millhaven will be their next stop. If players refuse the politeness drops — not into rage, just into the flat practicality of a man with twelve armed soldiers behind him who has stopped having the energy for negotiation.

_"I don't want trouble. But I need those supplies and I will have them one way or another. That's just where we are."_

---

**The Soldiers**

Twelve total including Crane. Tired, hungry, functional. They are not demons, not monsters — they are men at the end of something who have made a bad choice with calm faces.

- **Crane** — CR 3, commands from the center, tactically experienced, will not retreat
- 3 priestess
- **5 regular soldiers** — CR 1 each, fight in formation, demoralized but disciplined
- **3 veteran soldiers** — CR 2 each, closer to Crane, more dangerous individually

Enough to be a serious fight. Not unwinnable but not clean either.

---

**The Complication**

If players let them go — or lose — Crane and his soldiers will reach Millhaven. The players know what is there. The stockpile those people have been building since the lord died, the first real security they have had in years. Crane will take what he decides he needs and call it legal. The villagers cannot stop him.

If players stop them here, Millhaven is safe. But these are Church soldiers — exhausted, desperate men on the wrong side of a bad decision, not demons. The players will have to decide what stopping them actually means.

---

**How to Run It**

Let the conversation breathe before anything happens. Crane is not in a hurry. He would genuinely prefer this to go smoothly. Give players room to negotiate, bluff, threaten, or find a third option. Combat is available but it should not feel like the only door.

If players mention Millhaven directly — that they've been there, that they know what the village has been through — Crane pauses. It doesn't change his decision but it changes his face for a moment. He is not a man without conscience. He is a man who has decided to ignore it.

---

**Point Moments (DM Only)**

- A player tries to negotiate and finds Crane is genuinely reasonable right up until he isn't
- A player mentions Millhaven and watches Crane's face
- A player lets them go and then has to decide whether to follow
- A player asks Crane what he thinks happens to Millhaven after he takes their supplies — reward whatever answer Crane gives and whatever the player does with it
- A player makes a decision that the rest of the party disagrees with — let the tension breathe
