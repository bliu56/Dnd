_Type: Atmosphere / Discovery_

---

**Setting the Scene**

The players smell it before they see it. Ash and something underneath ash — the specific quality of destruction that has been sitting for weeks. When the city comes into view the picture is immediate and clear: demons did this. The scale of it, the totality, the way nothing was spared. Claw marks on the stones that survived. Demon corpses in the rubble. Church soldier bodies in the streets. The story writes itself and it is the obvious story.

There is nobody alive here. Whatever happened, happened long enough ago that anyone who was going to leave already did. The city is silent in the specific way that means it has been silent for a while.

---

**What Players See**

A destroyed city. Demon corpses. Church soldier corpses. Collapsed buildings. And somewhere near the center, a blast point — large, circular, the buildings around it simply gone rather than collapsed. At first glance it fits. A demon of sufficient power could produce something like this. The eye moves past it without stopping because the rest of the scene is already filling in the explanation.

---

**Pulling the Thread**

Players who stop and look will find the scene holds together on the surface. Players who look harder will find it starts to loosen.

The investigation is layered — each successful check reveals one more thing that doesn't quite fit, but none of it is conclusive alone. It only becomes clear when players put multiple pieces together.

_DC 14 Investigation — The Blast Point_ The blast originated from a single fixed point underground or at ground level. Not from above, not from multiple sources. One origin. Demon attacks spread outward from multiple points of entry. This didn't.

_DC 16 Investigation — The Demon Bodies_ The demon corpses near the blast point are not killed by conventional weapons. The damage to them is concussive — internal, consistent with an explosion rather than combat. Something killed them from the inside out. Demons don't do that to each other.

_DC 16 Perception — The Soldier Bodies_ Church soldiers died in two distinct patterns. Those near the walls and barricades died in combat — wounds consistent with demon attacks. Those near the center died differently. Concussive damage. Same as the demons. They were inside when it went off.

_DC 18 Investigation — The Barricades_ Some of the barricades near the city gates were constructed from the inside facing outward. Not to keep demons out. To keep people in.

**Putting It Together** No single check tells the full story. Players who make multiple checks and compare what they found will arrive at a picture that doesn't match the demon narrative. Something deliberate happened here. Something that killed demons and civilians and Church soldiers indiscriminately from a single point. The players have no name for it yet. Just the shape of it, wrong underneath the obvious explanation.

---

**Point Moments (DM Only)**

- A player stops at the blast point without being prompted and starts looking
- A player puts two or more findings together out loud and arrives somewhere uncomfortable
- A player decides not to investigate and just keeps moving — that's a valid choice, and the truth will find them later anyway
- A player finds the inward-facing barricades and goes quiet
