_Location: 2-B — Reached from the bandit road or the open plains._

---

**Trading Outpost (unnamed)**

This was never meant to be a settlement. It started as a single tent where a refugee family stopped to rest. Then another joined. Then three more. Now it's a cluster of mismatched structures — salvaged wood, repurposed wagon sides, canvas patched with leather. No walls. No gate. Just a collection of people who had nowhere else to go and decided to stay.

---

**The People**

- Humans only. Not by rule — by circumstance. No demon has ever come here, and no one is sure if that's coincidence or something unspoken.
- Everyone lost their home. They don't talk about where they came from. They talk about what's next.
- Slightly more resentful toward the Church than toward demons. The Church was supposed to protect them. It didn't.

---

**The Mood**

- Practical. Hard. Not hostile — but nobody smiles easily.
- Trade happens in the open. Goods, information, rumors. No one asks where things came from.
- Night is quiet. People keep to themselves. Strangers are watched.

---

**NPCs**

**Kellan** — Runs the central trading area.

A former quartermaster for a merchant caravan that got burned out. He's the closest thing the outpost has to organization. He sits behind a wide wooden counter (salvaged from a church pew) and keeps a mental ledger of who owes what. He doesn't trust easily, but he's fair. If you trade honestly with him once, he'll remember.

> *"I don't care where you came from or where you're going. I care what you're carrying and what you're willing to part with."*

---

**Rina** — The scout.

A lean woman in her thirties with a permanent squint from years of watching distant horizons. She lost her family to a demon raid — not the Demon King's army, just a rogue pack. She hates demons but has the honesty to say the Church didn't lift a finger either. She runs messages and small cargo between the outpost and anyone willing to pay. She knows the roads better than anyone here.

> *"I've buried people on every road between here and the Peaks. If you want to know which way is safe, I'm your only option."*

---

**Vasili** — The oddities seller.

An old man with a stall at the edge of the outpost. No one knows where he came from. He trades in things people found on the road and couldn't identify — old coins with unknown sigils, a sealed vial of black liquid, a compass that points somewhere other than north, a journal written in a language no one reads. He doesn't know what any of it does. He's just selling what he found. He prices everything the same: "Whatever you think it's worth."

He might have something genuinely valuable. He might have junk. He genuinely doesn't know.

> *"Found this one near a burned wagon. Could be treasure. Could be a curse. Only one way to find out."*

---

**[[Sera]]** — The outpost's unofficial record-keeper.

A quiet woman who lost her husband and son to the Church's conscription. She can't read or write, but she remembers everything — faces, names, routes, who passed through when, what they were carrying, which direction they went. She sits near the well and watches. If the players need information about someone or something that passed through, she's the one to ask.

She doesn't offer information unprompted. But if someone asks the right question, she answers without hesitation.

> *"The Church runner? Three days ago. Heading south. Looked like he'd been riding hard. Didn't stop to trade."*

**Potential Hooks**

- A runner from a nearby Church garrison arrived this morning. The outpost is divided on whether to hear them out or turn them away.
- Vasili has something new today — something he doesn't recognize. It might be the only one of its kind.
- Rina is planning a scouting run toward the Mid Point. She could use an escort.
- Sera saw someone pass through recently. Someone the players might recognize from their past.
- If the Hero Party visits: Kael won't even dismount. He'll wait at the edge of the outpost and send Finn or Maren in to gather supplies. Isolde might try to preach to the survivors — it won't go well.
