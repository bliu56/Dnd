_Location: 2-A — Reached from Twin-Moon Lake or through the hills beyond the bandit road._

---

**Stillwater Hollow**

A village built around a spring-fed pool so clear you can see the bottom no matter how deep it goes. The water is cold year-round. The village is old — older than the Church's presence in this region — and they keep to themselves.

---

**Atmosphere**

- The village is small — maybe thirty structures huddled around the pool. Stone and timber, low roofs, moss on everything that doesn't move.
- No walls. No gate. The village doesn't need them — nothing has ever threatened Stillwater Hollow, and the villagers seem to believe that's not a coincidence.
- The air smells of cold stone and wet earth. The only sound is the water — a slow, constant trickle from the spring. Voices carry farther here than they should.
- Villagers move slowly. Not out of laziness — out of habit. Nothing in Stillwater Hollow is done in a hurry.
- No Church presence. No Church symbols. No altar to any known god. The villagers have their own way of marking the sacred, and they do not speak of it to outsiders.

---

**The Pool and the Blade**

The spring-fed pool is the heart of the village. It's roughly thirty feet across, circular, lined with smooth pale stone that was laid so long ago no one remembers who set it. The water is so clear that every pebble on the bottom is visible — but the bottom is deeper than it looks. Estimates range from twenty to sixty feet, and no villager has ever bothered to confirm.

At the center of the pool, a sword is embedded in a stone that rises just above the waterline. The stone is dark grey, veined with silver, worn smooth by centuries of rain and touch. The blade itself is unremarkable — no gems, no inscriptions, no glow. It looks like a perfectly ordinary sword that happened to be driven into a rock and forgotten.

No one knows who put it there. No one knows how long it's been there. The blade chooses its wielder at random — there is no test, no worthiness, no logic. Anyone can walk into the pool and try to pull it. Most feel nothing. A rare few are chosen, and when they are, they hear the voices of spirits bound into the blade — fragments of old memory, not guidance.

The villagers do not worship the sword. They simply live alongside it, as they always have.

---

**Kaelen**

A silver-white spirit fish with one deep-water eye and one pale-mist eye. It is the oldest known inhabitant of the village — older than the eldest elder, older than the sword, older than the spring itself. It surfaces only when the sword is about to speak. No one has ever caught it. No one has ever tried.

---

**The Spirit of the Blade**

When the sword chooses someone, a woman's form manifests above the water. She does not speak in full sentences. She *remembers* — fragments of things long forgotten. Events no living person witnessed. Names that were erased from history. She is not an oracle. She is a record.

---

**The Villagers**

The people of Stillwater Hollow number about seventy — across maybe fifteen households. They fish the spring's outflow stream, keep goats on the rocky hillsides, grow hardy vegetables in thin soil. They trade cured fish and woven reed baskets with passing caravans. They do not seek contact with the outside world, but they do not refuse it either. Strangers are treated with quiet courtesy and quiet watchfulness.

They have no formal leader. When decisions need to be made, the village gathers at the pool's edge and talks until consensus forms. This can take hours. This can take days. They are in no hurry.

---

**NPCs**

**Elder Mira** — The oldest living villager. Speaker by default.

Mira is past eighty, thin as a reed, with eyes that have been watching the water for longer than anyone in the party has been alive. She moves slowly and deliberately, as if every motion has been considered in advance. She is the one who greets outsiders — not because she was elected, but because she has the patience for it that younger villagers lack.

She does not smile easily, but she is not cold. She is simply *present*, in a way that makes people around her slow down without noticing.

> *"You are welcome to stay as long as you need. The water does not ask where you came from. Neither do we."*

She will answer questions about the blade honestly, but briefly. She does not embellish. She does not speculate. She states what she knows and stops.

> *"It chooses. That is all I can tell you. It has chosen farmers, children, and warriors. It has passed over kings. There is no pattern. It simply decides."*

If asked about Kaelen surfacing:
> *"The fish does what the fish does. It is not for us to interpret. We only watch."*

Beneath the calm, Mira is paying close attention to the party. She has lived long enough to recognize when something important is about to happen, and she can feel it in the water.

---

**Torben** — The water-tender. Young, nervous, earnest.

Torben is twenty-two, born and raised in Stillwater Hollow. His job is to maintain the stone platform around the blade — clearing debris, scrubbing moss, making sure nothing obscures the pool's clarity. It is not a prestigious role, but it is one he takes seriously.

He is the one who first noticed Kaelen had surfaced. He was at the pool at dawn, as always, and saw the fish circling the blade — slow, deliberate, its pale-mist eye fixed on something he couldn't see. He ran to tell Mira. He has not slept since.

> *"I've never seen it before. I mean, I've heard the stories my whole life, but I never thought — I didn't think it would happen while I was the one watching."*

He is eager to talk to the party, partly because they're new and interesting, partly because he's scared and doesn't know how to say it. If the party shows any interest in the blade, he'll hover nearby, hoping to witness something.

> *"Are you going to try it? The sword? You don't have to. No one has to. But if you want to — I mean, I could show you the way in. The water's cold, but it's not dangerous."*

---

**Vena** — The one the blade touched once.

Vena is in her late fifties. She has lived in Stillwater Hollow her entire life. When she was twelve, she walked into the pool on a dare from the other village children and touched the blade. She was not chosen — the sword did not move — but she felt something: a single word, pressed into her mind like a stone dropped into still water.

*Wait.*

She has been waiting for forty-five years. She does not know what she is waiting for. She has built a life around the possibility that one day, she will understand.

> *"It said 'wait.' One word. I've turned it over in my head every day since. I used to think it meant I was meant for something. Now I think it might have just been — the only thing the sword had to say to a twelve-year-old girl."*

She is not bitter. She is not obsessed. She simply carries the word with her, the way other people carry old letters or folded photographs. She will not ask the party to try the blade, but she will watch them carefully if they do. She will not say anything if nothing happens. But if something *does* happen — if the blade stirs, or the spirit appears — she will step forward, trembling, and ask:

> *"Did it say anything? To you? Even one word?"*

---

**Doran** — Fisherman. Practical. Unimpressed.

Doran is in his forties, broad-shouldered, perpetually half-dried from a morning on the water. He is the village's most vocal skeptic about the blade — not because he disbelieves in its power, but because he thinks the village has spent too many generations staring at a rock when there are fish to catch and roofs to repair.

> *"That sword has been in that stone since before anyone's great-grandparents were born. It'll be there after we're all dead. In the meantime, people need to eat."*

He is not hostile to outsiders, but he is blunt. He will tell the party that the road east is dangerous, that the war has made everything harder, that the Church tried to recruit from Stillwater Hollow once and was politely told no.

> *"They sent a priest. Nice enough fellow. Told us the goddess would protect us. I asked him — has she protected anyone yet? He didn't have an answer."*

Despite his gruffness, Doran is the most likely to offer practical help — a place to sleep, directions, a meal. He does not believe in grand destinies, but he believes in being useful.

---

**Liss** — Restless. Almost chosen. Wants to leave.

Liss is nineteen. She tried the blade three weeks ago, not because she believed she would be chosen, but because she was bored — bored of the same water, the same faces, the same quiet life she has known since birth. She waded into the pool, grabbed the hilt, and pulled.

Nothing happened. The blade did not move.

But the water rippled. Rings spread from the stone — slow, deliberate, in perfect concentric circles — even though there was no wind, no movement, nothing to explain them. The ripples continued for nearly a minute after she let go.

The villagers did not know what to make of it. Liss does not know what to make of it. She only knows that ever since, she has felt like she is standing on the edge of something she cannot see.

> *"It didn't choose me. I know that. But — something happened. I felt it in my chest. Like a door opened and then closed before I could see what was on the other side."*

She wants to leave Stillwater Hollow. She has wanted to leave for years. Now she has a reason she cannot explain, and it is driving her toward restlessness. If the party seems interesting or capable, she will ask to join them — not because she is a fighter (she is not), but because she cannot bear to stay.

> *"Take me with you. I don't care where you're going. I just need to go somewhere. Please."*

Whether the party takes her or not, her desperation is genuine. If they refuse, she will find another way — or try the blade again.

---

**Old Jorl** — Remembers the last choosing.

Jorl is the second-oldest person in the village, after Mira. He is in his eighties, nearly blind, and spends most of his days sitting on a bench near the pool, facing the water even though he can barely see it. He was thirteen when the sword last chose someone.

> *"A woman. Traveler. Came through with a group heading east. She didn't even want to try — her companions talked her into it. She walked in, took the hilt, and the blade slid out like it was made of water."*

He remembers the spirit appearing — a woman's form rising from the pool, speaking in a language no one understood. The chosen woman listened, nodded once, and walked away. She died three days later, killed by something on the road. The blade returned to the stone on its own before her body was cold.

> *"The sword does not choose people to save them. It chooses people because it has something to say. Whether they survive hearing it — that is not the blade's concern."*

He tells this story without drama. Just fact. He has had seventy years to come to terms with it.

---

**Hero Party Reactions**

**Kael** will walk to the pool's edge, study the blade, and reach for it without asking permission. If Finn or Maren try to warn him, he will ignore them. He will grip the hilt, pull, and feel nothing. He will stand there for a long moment, face unreadable, then let go and walk away without a word. He will not speak of it. He will not try again.

**Isolde** will be visibly unsettled. The blade's power is not from Aelura — it is older, stranger, and she can feel it. She will not touch the water. She will watch from a distance, and if a player tries the blade, she will watch them with an expression that shifts between curiosity and wariness. She will not try to recruit anyone here. The air feels wrong to her.

**Gareth** will shrug, walk in, and try the blade. When nothing happens, he will say: *"Huh. Worth a shot."* and go back to dry land without a second thought. He will not be affected by the outcome either way.

**Seraphine** will not touch the blade. She will sit at the edge of the pool and study it — not the sword itself, but the water, the stone, Kaelen if it surfaces, the ripples, the way light bends through the spring. She will take notes. She will ask Torben detailed questions about the water's temperature, depth, and mineral content. She will treat the entire phenomenon as a research subject, not a mystical experience.

---

**Potential Hooks**

- The sword has been silent for an unusually long time. The villagers are uneasy — the longest gap between choosings was fifty years, and it has now been sixty-seven.
- Kaelen surfaced last night — circling the blade for the first time in living memory. The villagers do not know what it means, but they know it means *something*.
- A player who touches the blade may feel nothing (most do), may feel a whisper, or — very rarely — may be chosen. If chosen, the spirit of the blade speaks. What it says should connect to the player's past, the Demon King, or Ashen Vale.
- Liss will try to leave with the party. Whether they take her or not, she will find a way out of Stillwater Hollow before the week is out.
- Old Jorl's story about the last chosen woman raises an uncomfortable question: did the blade tell her something that got her killed?
- Doran can tell the party about the road ahead — what's between Stillwater Hollow and the Mid Point — for the price of a good meal and honest company.
