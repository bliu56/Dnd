_Location: 1-A — The path less traveled, through open country._

---

**Twin-Moon Lake**

A vast, still lake that reflects the sky so perfectly it's difficult to tell where the water ends and the air begins. At night, the moon appears twice — one above, one below — and locals say both are equally real.

---

**Atmosphere**

- The lake is unnervingly quiet. No birds. No wind on the water.
- Fishermen from nearby settlements tell stories of pulling up empty hooks — as if something cuts the line before they can see what bit.
- At dawn and dusk, the water is completely flat. Nothing moves on its surface or beneath it.
- The shore is lined with pale grey stone. The only structure is a small, weathered shack — home to the lake's only permanent resident.

---

**The Fish — Lux & Umbra**

The lake is home to a pair of identical fish. They look exactly alike — silver scales, pale fins, no visible difference. One, **Lux**, grants vitality and healing to whoever eats it. The other, **Umbra**, kills them instantly. No one has found a way to tell them apart before consumption. As a result, the fish are considered a myth or a trap — people talk about them, but no one is desperate enough to test their luck.

---

**NPCs**

**Old Ren** — The lone fisherman.

Old Ren has lived at Twin-Moon Lake for as long as anyone can remember. No one knows where he came from. No one asks. His shack sits at the lake's edge — a lean-to of salvaged wood and patched canvas, held together by stubbornness. He fishes the lake every day. He never catches anything.

> *"I've been casting into this water for thirty years. Never pulled up a single fish. Not one. And I'm still here. What does that tell you?"*

He knows the legends of Lux and Umbra. He claims he's seen them both — circling each other in the deep water, moving in perfect sync, identical in every way. He says he could never bring himself to try catching one, because if he did, he'd have to decide whether to eat it. And he's not ready to make that choice.

> *"Some questions ain't meant to be answered. That fish — whichever one it is — it's askin' a question. And I don't trust myself to give the right answer."*

He is not hostile, but he is guarded. He will share stories and rumors over a fire, but he will not answer personal questions. If pressed, he goes quiet and looks at the water until the question passes.

**If the party shows interest in the fish:**

Old Ren will watch them carefully. He will not stop them from trying to catch one, but he will offer one piece of advice:

> *"If you catch 'em both — you still won't know which is which. That's the trick. Even side by side, they're the same. The only way to find out is to take a bite. And by then it's too late."*

If a player catches one of the fish and is about to eat it, Old Ren will watch. He will not intervene. He will not warn them again. He will simply watch, because he wants to know the answer as much as anyone.

---

**What's Actually in the Lake**

The fish are real. Both of them. They are not magical constructs — they are natural creatures, impossibly rare, that have lived in this lake since before the lake had a name. There are exactly two of them. There have always been exactly two.

No one has ever caught both at once. The lake does not permit it — the moment one is hooked, the other vanishes into the depths, unreachable, until the first is either eaten or released.

If a player catches one and tries to release it, the fish will swim in a slow circle near the shore, as if waiting to see what happens next.

---

**Potential Hooks**

- Someone at the lakeshore has caught one of the fish and is trying to decide whether to eat it.
- A local believes they can tell the difference based on a small quirk in how the fish moves. They're willing to bet their life on it.
- The fish have not been seen in weeks — Old Ren is worried. Something has changed beneath the surface.
- Old Ren might know something about the road ahead, but he won't share it for free. He trades in stories, not coin.
- If the party catches and eats a fish: Lux grants 2d8+4 temporary hit points that last until the next long rest. Umbra deals 6d10 poison damage, Constitution save DC 15 for half.
