_Scene: Seraphine emerges from her tent. She does not want to be bothered — unless you have something interesting to say._

---

**If the players stayed at camp:**

The camp is quiet after Gareth left. A few minutes pass. Then the tent flap shifts.

Seraphine emerges slowly — not groggy, just deliberate. Her hair is slightly disheveled, her robes pulled on but not fastened with her usual precision. She blinks at the morning light like she's calculating whether it's worth acknowledging.

She sees the players still at camp. Pauses.

**Seraphine:** "...You're still here."

Not a question. She moves toward the fire, accepts a cup from Maren without looking at her, and sits apart from everyone else. A worn journal lands on her knee. She opens it and reads.

---

**If the players went with Gareth:**

When the training session winds down, as the players catch their breath, someone notices Seraphine sitting on a fallen tree at the edge of the clearing. Already there. Cup beside her. Journal in her lap. She doesn't look up.

---

**Seraphine's default state:**

She does not initiate conversation. If a player tries to talk to her:

**Player:** (anything casual)
**Seraphine:** "Mm." (doesn't look up)

**Player:** (another attempt)
**Seraphine:** "...What?"

**Player:** (small talk, weather, how she slept)
**Seraphine:** (flat stare) "Is there a point to this?"

She is not interested in pleasantries. She is not interested in bonding. She is interested in her notes.

---

**The only way in:**

If a player mentions something intellectually unique — an unusual spell, a creative use of an ability, a magical theory, a tactical observation she hasn't considered — she stops. Her pen pauses. She looks up.

**Seraphine:** "...Say that again."

If the player elaborates and it's genuinely interesting, she closes her journal. Full attention.

**Seraphine:** "I hadn't considered that. That's — actually interesting." (a pause) "Where did you learn that?"

From here, she will actually talk. Not warmly. Not socially. But she will engage. The conversation stays on the topic — she won't let it drift into personal territory. But if the player keeps bringing interesting ideas, she keeps engaging.

---

**The moment she gets bored:**

If the conversation loses steam or the player runs out of substance, she notices immediately.

**Seraphine:** "...That's all you've got on that?"

Beat.

**Seraphine:** (opening her journal again) "Let me know if you think of something else."

Back to her notes. The window is closed until the next interesting idea.

---

**If the Demon King is mentioned:**

Seraphine's pen stops.

She does not look up immediately. She sits still for a long moment — the kind of stillness that means she's deciding how to handle what she just heard.

Then she closes her journal. Slowly. Sets it aside.

**Seraphine:** (quiet) "What did you just say?"

She looks at the player who said it. Her voice is flat, but her eyes are sharp — locked on, not blinking.

**If the player confirms they met him:**

She stands. The journal on the tree is forgotten.

**Seraphine:** "You met him. You *spoke* to him. And you are just now mentioning this?"

She steps closer.

**Seraphine:** "When. Where. What did he look like. What did he say. Did he know who you were. Did he try to kill you. Did he offer you anything. Did he let you leave or did you escape. *Tell me everything.*"

She doesn't raise her voice, but the questions come in a rapid, controlled stream — like she's been waiting for this information and is not going to let a single detail slip past.

**If the player hesitates or deflects:**

**Seraphine:** "No. You don't get to hold this back. I have been trying to understand him for two years. Two years of reports, patterns, secondhand accounts from dead soldiers. And you — you *talked* to him. So you're going to tell me everything you remember, and then you're going to tell me everything you think you forgot, and then we're going to go over it again until I'm satisfied."

She crosses her arms.

**Seraphine:** "Start talking."

She will not let this go. She follows the player around for the rest of the morning if she has to. This is the single most valuable piece of intelligence she could have gotten, and she treats it as such.

---

**After the interrogation:**

Once she has everything she can get, she sits back down. Her journal is open again, but she's not reading — she's writing. Compiling. Cross-referencing.

**Seraphine:** (without looking up) "...This changes things. I need to recalculate."

A pause.

**Seraphine:** "...Thank you. That was — useful. I mean that."

It's the closest thing to gratitude she'll ever show. Then she goes back to her notes and doesn't say another word until she's done processing.

---

**Tone Notes (DM Only)**

- Seraphine is not rude for the sake of being rude. She just genuinely does not value interaction that doesn't produce useful information.
- A player who catches her interest once will find her slightly less dismissive next time — not warm, but she'll remember them.
- The threshold for "interesting" should be high but reachable. A creative spell combo, a smart tactical read, a piece of lore the player figured out ahead of time.
- If a player tries to fake it or repeat something they don't understand, she'll see through it immediately. She respects genuine intelligence, not performance.
- The Demon King interrogation is her highest-priority conversation. If a player mentions him, everything else Seraphine was doing is now secondary.
