# Hewn — The Architect

## Overview

A prisoner who carved an underground city with his bare hands over 40 years. Died at completion.

---

## Their Life

Hewn was sentenced to life in a underground prison — a natural cave system that had been converted into a holding facility. There was no work detail, no rehabilitation, no point. Prisoners were left to rot in the dark.

Hewn refused to rot. He started carving.

With his bare hands, then with sharpened stones, then with tools he made from the rock itself, he carved. A cell became a passage. A passage became a hall. A hall became a chamber. He worked obsessively — not out of hope, but out of the need to do *something* with the time he had left.

Forty years. An underground city — streets, plazas, a cathedral of stone, all carved from nothing. Other prisoners joined him. Some found purpose. Some found peace. Hewn found neither — he only found the next wall.

When the final structure was complete — a great dome at the city's heart — he sat beneath it, looked up at his own hands, and died. The prison had long since been abandoned. The city he built was his alone.

---

## How the Collector Touched It

The Collector gave him endless endurance — the physical capacity to keep going long after any normal body would have failed. He watched Hewn's mind slowly fragment in the dark, watched the obsession consume everything else. Collected at the moment the last stone was set.
