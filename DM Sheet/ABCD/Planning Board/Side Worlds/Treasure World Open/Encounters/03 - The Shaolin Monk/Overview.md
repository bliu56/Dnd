**Monk Wuchen (无尘) — The Shaolin Monk**

*Shaolin Temple — First Tier (One of the Three Peaks)*
*High Martial Disciple at best*

---

## Who He Is

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Wuchen — "no dust" |
| **Sect** | Shaolin Temple, first-tier. One of the **Three Peaks** |
| **Level** | High Martial Disciple at best — young, not yet a master |
| **Master** | Abbot Huiming (Transcendent — one of the realm's strongest) |
| **Nature** | Calm, absolute conviction. Not malicious — genuinely believes he's preventing bloodshed |

**His view:** The treasure will inspire greed in others. Keeping it causes bloodshed. Taking it minimizes violence. He is doing a kindness.

---

## The Encounter

The party comes across a treasure that seems strong/powerful. The monk approaches and asks if he can take it away **for safekeeping**.

His reasoning is sincere: this treasure will draw wolves. Blood will be spilled over it. If he takes it, no one dies over it — he will see it sealed or returned where it cannot cause harm.

If the party refuses, he will take it **by force** if needed. Not with anger — with sorrow. But he will not yield.

## Lines

| Moment | Lines |
|---|---|
| **Approach** | *"Peace be with you, travelers. I see you have found a treasure that is not meant to be kept."* |
| **The request** | *"May I take it into safekeeping? I swear by my temple — I will not keep it for myself. But if it remains here, it will draw those who would kill for it."* |
| **On greed** | *"I have seen what treasures do to men. They whisper. They corrupt. It is kinder to remove the temptation than to watch it devour the tempted."* |
| **If refused** | *(a long, sad pause)* *"Then I am sorry. But I cannot allow this blood to be shed. Please — do not make me take it from you."* |
| **If they fight** | *"I did not wish for this. I will pray for your souls as I end this."* |
| **On his faith** | *"I am not here for glory. I am here so that fewer people die for things that cannot love them back."* |
| **If they see through him** | *(a hint of genuine surprise)* *"...You are wise. Very few see that I speak the truth."* |
| **After taking it** | *"May the Buddha forgive me for what I have taken. And may he forgive you for what you almost did."* |

---

## DM Notes

- He is sincere — the party *can* see through him with a good Insight check. The tragedy is that sincerity doesn't make him less dangerous
- He fights with restraint — non-lethal where possible. He's not a murderer, but he will not lose
- As a high Disciple of the strongest sect, he carries Shaolin's reputation — hurting him badly has consequences outside the realm
- The treasure's fate is a hook: if they let him take it, they might meet him again — and find out what he actually did with it
