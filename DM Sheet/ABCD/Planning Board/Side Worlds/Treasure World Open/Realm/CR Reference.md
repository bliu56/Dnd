**CR Reference — Martial Levels**

How the martial ladder maps to D&D Challenge Rating. Use this to place NPCs relative to the party.

| Martial Level | Approx. CR | Comparison |
|---|---|---|
| **Low Disciple** | CR 1–2 | Basic sect recruit, guards, common thugs |
| **Mid Disciple** | CR 3–4 | Trained fighter, competent but replaceable |
| **High Disciple** | CR 5–6 | Strong young talent, sect's future |
| **Low Master** | CR 7–8 | Serious threat, sect elite |
| **Mid Master** | CR 9–10 | Party's equal or slightly above |
| **High Master** | CR 11–12 | Fights above the party's weight |
| **Grandmaster** | CR 13–15 | Boss-tier, a fight the party plans around |
| **Transcendent** | CR 16–20+ | Beyond fighting — plot device tier |

---

## Reference Points

- **Xiao Yun** (mid Disciple) — **CR 4**. Competent, not exceptional.
  - Decisively beats normal bandits and lower-tier sect members
  - One player of the party's level can handle her — but she's not a joke
  - Clearly below the party (they fight CR 7+ demons)
  - Two or three of her = a fair skirmish for a level 7–9 party
  - If she grows to **Master** (via the party's intangible gifts), she becomes CR 7–8 — a real peer
- **Master Chen Weiming** (Master, at least) — **CR 8–9**
- **Lin Shuang** (low-mid Master) — **CR 8**
- **Old Xu** (Grandmaster) — **CR 13–14** — a genuine "do not start this fight" wall
