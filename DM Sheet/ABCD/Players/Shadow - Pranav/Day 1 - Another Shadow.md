Major: Musical History Major

The group project was assigned on a Tuesday, which meant by Wednesday the other three had already decided it was someone else's problem.

Shadow sat at the library table and watched them. One was on his phone. One was whispering to the other about something that had nothing to do with the assignment. The third hadn't shown up at all. She had expected this. She did not know why she had expected anything different.

She opened her laptop and began.

The only other person who seemed to understand that the project was, in fact, a project was the guy across the table from her. Slightly built, unremarkable in most ways except that he was not completely unpleasant to look at, which she noted and then set aside. He had his own laptop open, his own notes out, his own quiet frustration sitting just behind his eyes every time he glanced at the others.

At some point he looked at her and said nothing. She said nothing back. They returned to their work. It was the most functional conversation she'd had all week.

By the end of the session, she had done the majority of it. He had done the rest. The other three had rearranged themselves into increasingly comfortable positions and contributed nothing of substance. No one thanked her. She did not expect them to.

She packed her bag, walked home, and did not think about any of them.

---

Sleep came quickly. It usually did when she was tired enough to stop thinking.

She was somewhere between dreaming and not when she felt it.

A hand. Cold in a way that had nothing to do with temperature — a cold that came from somewhere else entirely, from somewhere that didn't have a name she knew. It moved across her face slowly. Almost gentle. The kind of touch that would have been tender if it had come from anything warm.

She surfaced.

The room was dark. The room was empty. She lay still for a moment, eyes open, waiting for her heartbeat to settle into something reasonable.

Then she heard it.

Breathing. Heavy and close and distinctly not hers. She turned her head. Nothing. She sat up. Nothing. The window was closed. The door was closed. The room held only the particular silence of a place that had just stopped pretending.

She sat on the edge of her bed for a long time.

She did not turn the light on. She already knew the light wouldn't show her anything.
